## Purpose
Distress tolerance skills help a person get through a very intense emotional moment without making things worse. The goal is not to solve the problem right away, but to ride out the wave safely until thinking clearly becomes possible again.

## Typically used when
- Emotions spike so strongly that the person feels they might act on urges they would later regret.
- The situation cannot be changed immediately.
- Someone has a pattern of reacting impulsively under extreme stress.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Cold water and intense exercise may not be suitable for people with heart conditions or eating difficulties; check with a qualified professional first.
- These skills are for short-term relief; they do not replace longer-term support.
- If there is immediate risk of harm, contact crisis support straight away.
- Not for pushing emotions away permanently.

## Steps
1. Notice and name that you are at a very high level of distress.
2. Pause before acting on any urge; remind yourself the feeling will rise and then fall.
3. Cool down: splash cool water on your face or hold something cold for a short while.
4. Use your body: walk briskly or move for a few minutes if it is safe for you.
5. Slow your breathing, making the out-breath longer than the in-breath.
6. Soothe your senses with something comforting: a familiar smell, soft fabric, or calming sound.
7. Once the intensity drops, decide on one helpful next step.

- feelings are too intense
- about to explode
