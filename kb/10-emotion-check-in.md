## Purpose
An emotion check-in is a short, regular pause to notice and name what one is feeling. Naming emotions accurately can make them easier to understand and respond to, and over time it reveals patterns linked to sleep, people, or situations.

## Typically used when
- A person often feels "bad" or "fine" without being able to say more.
- Emotions seem to arrive suddenly and feel confusing.
- Someone wants to track mood between sessions to discuss later.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Checking in should not become constant monitoring that raises anxiety; two or three times a day is plenty.
- Some people find naming emotions very hard at first; offer a simple list of feeling words.
- If a check-in reveals thoughts of self-harm, stop and reach out for support immediately.
- Do not use ratings to judge oneself as doing well or badly.

## Steps
1. Pause for a minute and take a slow breath.
2. Notice sensations in your body: tension, warmth, heaviness, restlessness.
3. Name the main emotion, choosing the closest word from a feelings list if needed.
4. Rate its strength from 0 to 10.
5. Note what was happening just before: place, people, thoughts.
6. Ask what this emotion might need from you right now, such as rest, connection, or action.
7. Record it briefly so you can look back at patterns later.
