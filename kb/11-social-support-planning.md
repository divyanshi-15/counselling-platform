## Purpose
People cope better when they feel connected. Social support planning helps someone identify who and what they can turn to, and plan small, realistic steps to reconnect or ask for help.

## Typically used when
- A person has become isolated or has lost touch with friends, family, or community.
- They feel they have to handle everything alone.
- They are going through a stressful period and need practical or emotional backup.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Some relationships are unsafe or harmful; never encourage contact with someone who causes harm.
- Respect privacy; the person decides what, and how much, to share.
- Social anxiety or stigma may make reaching out hard; start with very low-pressure contact.
- Consider cultural and family expectations around asking for help.

## Steps
1. Draw three circles: people closest to you, people you know somewhat, and wider community or services.
2. Write names or groups in each circle, including teachers, neighbours, faith groups, or helplines.
3. Mark who offers emotional support, practical help, or simply good company.
4. Choose one person or group you would feel comfortable contacting this week.
5. Decide what you will do: a message, a call, a short visit, or joining an activity.
6. Plan what you might say, if that helps.
7. Afterwards, note how it went and choose a next step.
