## Purpose
A wind-down routine creates a buffer between the busy day and sleep. Doing the same calm activities in the same order each evening helps the mind and body recognise that it is time to slow down.

## Typically used when
- A person goes straight from work, screens, or chores into bed and feels "wired."
- Thoughts race at bedtime.
- Evenings are irregular and bedtimes vary widely.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- A routine supports sleep but does not fix everything; persistent sleep problems should be discussed with a qualified professional.
- Avoid making it so long or strict that it becomes another source of pressure.
- Shared living spaces, caregiving duties, or night shifts may require a simpler, flexible version.
- Respect cultural and family practices around evenings.

## Steps
1. Pick a target time to start winding down, about 45 to 60 minutes before bed.
2. Dim the lights and put work and stimulating screens aside.
3. Spend five minutes writing down tomorrow's tasks or any worries to set them aside.
4. Choose one or two calming activities: light stretching, a warm shower, gentle music, or reading something light.
5. Keep caffeine and heavy meals for earlier in the day.
6. Follow the same order of steps each night so it becomes familiar.
7. Go to bed when you feel sleepy, and keep your wake-up time steady.
