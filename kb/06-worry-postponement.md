## Purpose
Worry postponement teaches that worry can be scheduled rather than obeyed the moment it appears. By saving worries for a set "worry time," a person often finds they have more freedom during the rest of the day.

## Typically used when
- Someone worries on and off all day and feels unable to switch it off.
- Worries jump between many topics.
- The person wants a practical way to contain worry without trying to suppress it.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Urgent, real problems that need action now should be handled now, not postponed.
- Avoid scheduling worry time close to bedtime.
- If worry involves thoughts of harming oneself, that is not something to postpone; seek support straight away.
- Postponing is a skill that builds gradually; slips are normal.

## Steps
1. Choose a daily 15 to 20 minute worry time, at the same place and time, not near bedtime.
2. When a worry pops up during the day, notice it and jot a word or two on a note.
3. Tell yourself you will come back to it at worry time, then return attention to what you were doing.
4. At worry time, read through your list.
5. For each worry, ask whether it still matters and whether there is anything you can do.
6. If yes, plan one small action; if not, let it go for now.
7. When the time is up, stop and move to an absorbing activity.
