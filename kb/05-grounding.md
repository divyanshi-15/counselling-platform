## Purpose
Grounding brings attention back to the present moment through the senses. It can help when someone feels swept away by panic, intense memories, or a sense of being disconnected from their surroundings.

## Typically used when
- A person feels spaced out, unreal, or "not here."
- Strong memories or flashbacks pull attention into the past.
- Anxiety is rising quickly and breathing exercises feel too hard.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Some sensory cues may themselves be distressing; let the person choose which senses to use.
- If someone is unsafe or in immediate danger, practical safety comes first.
- Frequent or severe disconnection deserves discussion with a qualified professional.
- Keep the tone gentle and invitational, never forced.

## Steps
1. Plant your feet on the floor and notice the contact with the ground.
2. Look around and name five things you can see.
3. Notice four things you can physically feel, such as clothing, a chair, or air on your skin.
4. Listen for three sounds, near or far.
5. Notice two things you can smell, or recall two smells you like.
6. Notice one thing you can taste, or take a sip of water.
7. Say the date, where you are, and that you are safe right now, if that is true.
