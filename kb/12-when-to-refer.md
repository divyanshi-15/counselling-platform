## Purpose
Self-help skills have limits. This entry outlines signs that a person needs more than skill-building, and how to move quickly to the right level of support. It is a safety reference, not an exercise.

## Typically used when
- Someone mentions thoughts of ending their life, harming themselves, or harming others.
- There are signs of being out of touch with reality, severe confusion, or not eating, drinking, or sleeping for days.
- A person is experiencing abuse or is in immediate danger.
- Difficulties are getting worse despite regular support.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Never delay urgent help to finish an exercise or session.
- Do not promise to keep risk to life secret.
- Do not attempt to assess medical or psychiatric conditions yourself; refer to qualified professionals.
- Stay calm and non-judgemental; avoid lecturing or arguing.

## Steps
1. Stop the current activity and give the person your full attention.
2. Ask directly and gently whether they are thinking of harming themselves; asking does not plant the idea.
3. If there is immediate danger, call emergency services on 112 and stay with the person if it is safe.
4. Share the Tele-MANAS helpline, 14416 (free, 24/7, multiple languages).
5. Help them contact a trusted person who can be with them.
6. Inform the responsible therapist straight away and record what happened.
7. Arrange a follow-up with a qualified professional as soon as possible.
