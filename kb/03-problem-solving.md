## Purpose
When problems pile up, they can feel like one heavy fog. Structured problem-solving breaks things into separate, workable pieces so a person can decide what they can influence and take a concrete first step.

## Typically used when
- Someone feels overwhelmed by several practical stressors at once, such as work, money, housing, or family duties.
- Worry keeps circling without leading to any action.
- The person is calm enough to think through options step by step.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Some problems cannot be solved by the person alone (for example, abuse or discrimination); focus on safety and support instead of self-blame.
- Not for use in the middle of a crisis.
- Keep the chosen step small to avoid adding pressure.
- Be sensitive to limited resources; suggested options must be realistic for the person's situation.

## Steps
1. List all the problems on your mind, without sorting them yet.
2. Split them into ones you can influence and ones you cannot.
3. Choose one problem you can influence that feels manageable to start with.
4. Describe it clearly and specifically in one or two sentences.
5. Brainstorm as many possible actions as you can, without judging them.
6. Weigh the pros and cons of the most realistic few and pick one.
7. Break it into small steps and decide when you will do the first one.
8. Afterwards, review what happened and adjust the plan.
