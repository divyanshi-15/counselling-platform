## Purpose
Low mood often leads people to do less, and doing less tends to keep mood low. Behavioural activation gently reverses that loop by planning small, meaningful activities and noticing how they feel, rather than waiting to feel motivated first.

## Typically used when
- A person has withdrawn from hobbies, chores, friends, or routines.
- Days feel empty, flat, or spent mostly in bed or on a screen.
- The person can manage small steps and would value a structured plan.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Start very small; overly ambitious plans can lead to a sense of failure.
- Check for physical limits, pain, or fatigue that need medical review first.
- Not a substitute for safety planning if someone expresses thoughts of self-harm.
- Avoid framing it as "just try harder."

## Steps
1. For two or three days, jot down what you do each hour and rate your mood from 0 to 10.
2. Notice which activities lift mood slightly and which drain it.
3. List activities that matter to you: small pleasures, tasks that give a sense of achievement, and connection with others.
4. Pick one or two easy ones and schedule them for a specific day and time.
5. Do the activity even if motivation is low, then rate your mood afterwards.
6. Review the week: what helped, what got in the way.
7. Gradually add or adjust activities, keeping steps realistic.
