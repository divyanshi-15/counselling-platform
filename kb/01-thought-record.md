## Purpose
A thought record helps a person slow down a moment of strong feeling and look at the thought behind it. Writing it out creates a little distance, so the thought can be checked rather than simply believed.

## Typically used when
- Someone notices a sudden drop in mood, a rush of anxiety, or anger they cannot explain.
- The same harsh or fearful thoughts keep returning in similar situations.
- The person can already name feelings and is ready to examine thinking patterns.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Not during acute distress or crisis; settle the body first with grounding or slow breathing.
- Avoid if it turns into self-criticism ("I'm thinking wrong again").
- Some situations are genuinely unfair or unsafe; the aim is balance, not talking someone out of real problems.
- Reading or writing load may be too high for some people; a spoken version works too.

## Steps
1. Write down the situation briefly: where, when, who was there.
2. Name the feeling(s) and rate how strong each was from 0 to 100.
3. Write the thought that went through your mind, word for word if possible.
4. List what supports that thought.
5. List what does not quite fit with it, or what a friend might say.
6. Write a more balanced thought that takes both lists into account.
7. Re-rate the feeling and note anything you might do next.
