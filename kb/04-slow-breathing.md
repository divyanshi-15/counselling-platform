## Purpose
Slow, steady breathing gives the body a signal that it can settle. It is a portable skill a person can use anywhere to take the edge off tension, so they can think more clearly about what to do next.

## Typically used when
- Someone notices a racing heart, tight chest, shallow breathing, or restlessness.
- Before or after a stressful event such as an exam, meeting, or difficult conversation.
- As a short daily practice to make the skill easier to reach for later.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- If focusing on the breath increases panic, switch to grounding through the senses instead.
- People with breathing or heart conditions should keep it gentle and stop if they feel dizzy or unwell.
- Do not force very long or deep breaths; comfortable is enough.
- It eases stress in the moment but is not a fix for the underlying problem.

## Steps
1. Sit or stand comfortably and let your shoulders drop.
2. Rest one hand on your belly if that helps you notice movement.
3. Breathe in gently through your nose for a count of about three.
4. Pause briefly without straining.
5. Breathe out slowly through your mouth for a count of about four or five, a little longer than the in-breath.
6. Continue for two to five minutes, letting the rhythm stay relaxed.
7. Notice how your body feels now compared with when you started.
