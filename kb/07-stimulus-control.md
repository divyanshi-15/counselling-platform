## Purpose
When someone spends many hours awake in bed, the bed can become linked with frustration and alertness rather than sleep. Stimulus control rebuilds the connection between bed and sleeping, using a few simple, consistent rules.

## Typically used when
- A person lies awake for long stretches at night, often feeling wide awake once in bed.
- They use the bed for scrolling, work, or worrying.
- Sleep timing varies a lot from day to day.

## Cautions / when NOT to use
- Getting out of bed can increase daytime sleepiness at first; take care with driving or operating machinery.
- People with suspected sleep disorders such as loud snoring with pauses in breathing, seizures, or bipolar experiences should be reviewed by a qualified professional before starting.
- Night-shift workers may need an adapted plan.
- Be mindful of safety when moving around in the dark, especially for older adults.

## Steps
1. Go to bed only when you feel sleepy, not just tired.
2. Use the bed only for sleep and intimacy; read, scroll, or watch elsewhere.
3. If you are not asleep after what feels like about 20 minutes, get up and go to another dim, quiet space.
4. Do something calm there until you feel sleepy, then return to bed.
5. Repeat steps 3 and 4 as needed.
6. Get up at the same time every morning, including weekends.
7. Avoid daytime naps, or keep any nap short and early.
